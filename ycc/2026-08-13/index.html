D: Effective rate × debt/GDP × GDP/revenue (right side). Why: Closes the loop: price of debt × quantity of debt × fiscal extraction constraint. i* = (I/R_max) / ((D/Y)(Y/R)). • Claim Expansion Ratio (CER): Δ(Government debt + Monetary liabilities) ÷ Δ Nominal GDP N: Additional dollars of sovereign + base-money claims created. D: Additional dollars of nominal output. Why: How many dollars of new nominal claims are created per dollar of economic output — deeper than Debt/GDP alone. • Central Bank Absorption: Fed Treasury holdings ÷ Marketable Treasury debt N: SOMA Treasury stock. D: Marketable Treasury stock. Why: Share of the debt ocean parked at the central bank (non-price-sensitive stock). • Private Flow / Absorber Container: Net private absorption requirement ÷ Private fixed-income capacity N: New water: net issuance needing private buyers. D: Container size: private FI capacity available to absorb duration. Why: Ownership stock (% private) says who owns the ocean; this flow/container ratio says how hard new issuance presses the marginal buyer. • Fiscal Carry Ratio: Net Interest Expense ÷ Federal Revenue N: Net interest the Treasury pays (the cash cost of past and current debt). D: Federal tax/revenue base that must service that interest. Why: Shows how much of the government's actual income is consumed by interest — tighter than interest/GDP alone. • Private Absorption Burden: Private Absorption Requirement ÷ GDP N: Net issuance − Fed purchases − foreign official purchases − other structural demand (what private balance sheets must take). D: GDP (economy-wide capacity scale). Why: Centerpiece flow metric — how much incremental private balance sheet is demanded each year. Stocks show who owns yesterday's debt; flows set today's yield. • Marginal Official Absorption: (Δ Fed holdings + Δ Foreign Official) ÷ Net Treasury issuance N: Change in official holdings over the period (can be negative under QT / FOG selling). D: Net new Treasury supply that needed a buyer. Why: The stock can look large while the marginal buyer has disappeared — that is when private clearing yields rise. • Fiscal Yield Tolerance / Market Clearing Gap: Market long yield − (Interest/Revenue band × Revenue ÷ Debt) N: Market clearing long yield (30Y preferred). D: Implied average funding rate if interest were capped at each interest/revenue band (15–35%). Why: Distance between market pricing and the government's fiscal constraint — shown as a surface, not one fake-precision 'sustainable' number. • Long-End Independence (LEI): 10Y (or 30Y) yield − Expected average short/policy path N: Observed long yield. D: Path of expected short rates embedded in the curve / Fed path. Why: Separates Fed-expectations from term-premium / supply / fiscal compensation. Fed easing while longs rise = long-end rebellion. • YCC Necessity Gap: (Fiscal pressure + Market pressure) ÷ Alternative adjustment capacity N: How hard financing + clearing have become (SFPI ingredients). D: Remaining room in growth, consolidation, inflation tolerance, foreign demand, domestic savings, Fed easing, maturity extension. Why: Asks how much adjustment capacity remains before YCC becomes economically useful — YCC as an output, not an assumption. What does this mean? · YCC glossary

Browser teaching layer — definition, formula, mental shortcut. Email stays clean.

CER-D · Debt Claim Expansion Ratio
Definition: How fast federal debt claims grow relative to nominal GDP.
Formula: Δ Federal debt ÷ Δ Nominal GDP
Mental shortcut: If CER-D > 1, debt claims are outrunning the economy that services them.
CER-M · Money Claim Expansion Ratio
Definition: How fast monetary claims grow relative to nominal GDP.
Formula: Δ Monetary claims ÷ Δ Nominal GDP
Mental shortcut: Negative CER-M means liquidity claims contracted while the economy grew.
CER · Total Claim Expansion
Definition: Combined debt + money claim expansion vs nominal GDP.
Formula: CER-D + CER-M (model definition)
Mental shortcut: Total claims pressure on the servicing base.
SAR · Structural Absorption Ratio
Definition: Share of incremental Treasury supply taken by less price-sensitive buyers.
Formula: Structural / official demand ÷ Net issuance
Mental shortcut: When SAR falls, more supply must clear through private price.
MPAR · Marginal Private Absorption Ratio
Definition: Burden on price-sensitive private capital vs its fixed-income capacity.
Formula: Private absorption requirement ÷ Private fixed-income capacity
Mental shortcut: How much of the marginal auction has to be bribed with yield.
FCG · Fiscal Carry Gap
Definition: Gap between market long yield and the yield consistent with a reference Interest/Revenue band.
Formula: Market 30Y − implied rate at 20% Interest/Revenue
Mental shortcut: Positive FCG = market demands more than the model's lower fiscal-carry comfort zone.
I/R · Federal Interest / Revenue
Definition: Share of federal revenue consumed by net interest.
Formula: Net interest expense ÷ Federal revenue
Mental shortcut: Cents of every revenue dollar already spoken for by interest.
WAM · Weighted Average Maturity
Definition: Average maturity of marketable Treasury debt.
Formula: Value-weighted maturity of outstanding marketable debt
Mental shortcut: Shorter WAM = faster transmission of market yields into interest expense.
SOMA · Fed Treasury Portfolio
Definition: Federal Reserve System Open Market Account holdings of Treasuries.
Formula: SOMA Treasury holdings (stock)
Mental shortcut: Fed duration absorption / QT pace lives here.
FIMA · Foreign & International Monetary Authorities Repo Facility
Definition: Fed facility letting foreign official institutions repo Treasuries for dollars.
Formula: FIMA usage / outstanding
Mental shortcut: Elevated FIMA = external liquidity management under stress.
SFPI · Sovereign Financing Pressure Index
Definition: Composite of fiscal carry, refinancing, absorption, and market-clearing stress.
Formula: Weighted stress components → 0–100
Mental shortcut: How hard it is to finance the sovereign at market clearing prices.
YMRI · Yield Management Response Index
Definition: How actively Treasury / Fed / external tools lean against duration pressure.
Formula: Weighted policy-response components → 0–100
Mental shortcut: Is policy absorbing pressure that markets are showing?
YNG · YCC Necessity Gap
Definition: Pressure vs remaining adjustment capacity before yield-curve control becomes useful.
Formula: (Fiscal + market pressure) ÷ escape-valve capacity
Mental shortcut: YCC as an output of exhausted alternatives — not an assumption.
